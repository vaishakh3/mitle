# Product principles

Milte is not a smaller Tinder. Its job is to create one safe, legible chance encounter and then get out of the way.

## What the research changed

### 1. Reduce performance and choice overload

Large dating-app catalogs encourage optimization, comparison, and repeated self-presentation. Pew's US research found substantial disappointment and unwanted behavior in online dating, while published research describes dating-app fatigue and burnout.

**Product response:** no photos, bios, follower signals, likes, swipe deck, ranking explanation, or chat. One match per day remains the core constraint—not an empty-state problem to “fix.”

### 2. Remove coordination without creating a blind date

Products such as Breeze and Timeleft demonstrate that people will accept a service choosing the social plan when the logistics are clear. The original concept had the right instinct, but it left too much uncertainty between mutual acceptance and arrival.

**Product response:** availability-aware matching, a clear one-hour ticket, public venue details after mutual consent, a time-gated recognition phrase, and four operational day-of statuses. Status solves “are they coming?” without opening a conversational backchannel.

### 3. Make safety part of the primary journey

RAINN recommends sharing plans, meeting in a populated public place, arranging independent transport, and keeping an exit plan. Tinder's “Share My Date” validates plan sharing as a mainstream safety behavior. India's official 112 service provides the current launch-market emergency action.

**Product response:** shareable plans, public-place and transport guidance, a first-class safety center, official emergency action, confidential active-match reporting, and immediate match dissolution after a report. Safety is reachable from the committed-date screen rather than hidden in settings.

### 4. Preserve mystery without withholding control

Anonymity is useful only when it removes prejudgment—not when it removes agency. People still need to know what will happen, when it will happen, what the other person can see, and how to leave.

**Product response:** progressive disclosure, explicit consent at each irreversible point, privacy-safe “how to spot me” hints, plain-language data behavior, private declines, a visible opt-out/pause control, and no public reputation score.

## Experience rules

1. Every screen should answer one question.
2. Mystery may hide identity; it must never hide safety or consequences.
3. Copy should feel warm and specific, not gamified or mystical at the expense of clarity.
4. No feature should increase time spent in the app unless it materially improves arrival, safety, or closure.
5. A “no” is private and easy. A report is confidential and immediate.
6. The other person never receives a stable identifier through the match experience.
7. Operational states replace pre-date chat; the active match never becomes a messaging thread.
8. A post-date continuation may cross the app only when both people choose it independently; it must never become an inbox, archive, or one-sided message request.
9. Venue selection uses current public-place data and requires no venue partnerships or operational approvals; an unverifiable result fails safely instead of becoming a fabricated venue.
